1. Word Embeddings: Continuous Meaning Spaces

Computers operate on discrete numerical arrays. Word embeddings map discrete words $w \in V$ to dense vectors in a continuous high-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}^d$.

Semantic Clustering & Vector Arithmetic

Words sharing semantic meaning cluster together. The geometry of this space captures analogical relationships:

$$ \vec{King} - \vec{Man} + \vec{Woman} \approx \vec{Queen} $$

Evolution of Embedding Modalities

  • Static Embeddings (Word2Vec, GloVe)
    Mapping function $\phi: V \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ is invariant. The vector for "bank" is mathematically identical in "river bank" and "money bank".
  • Contextual Embeddings (BERT, GPT)
    Mapping is a function of the entire sequence context $\phi: (w_i, w_{i}) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$. Resolves polysemy by generating dynamic representations.

2. The RNN Bottleneck: Sequential Compression

In classical Encoder-Decoder Recurrent Neural Networks, the sequence is processed via a recurrent update function: $h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t)$.

Information Dilution (The Bottleneck)

The entire source sequence of length $T$ must be compressed into a single context vector $h_T \in \mathbb{R}^d$. Early token signals $x_1, x_2$ undergo severe dilution as $T \to \infty$.

Vanishing Gradients & Sequential Depth

Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) requires computing products of Jacobians $\prod_{i=k}^{T} \frac{\partial h_i}{\partial h_{i-1}}$. This recursive chain rule causes gradients to vanish, severely limiting the learning of long-range dependencies over an $O(T)$ sequential path.

3. The Formal Definition of Attention

Attention resolves the bottleneck by allowing $O(1)$ parallel access to all tokens. It is fundamentally an attention pooling mechanism over a database of Key-Value pairs.

Attention Pooling Formulation

Given a query $q$ and a database of key-value pairs $\mathcal{D} = \{(k_1, v_1), ..., (k_m, v_m)\}$, the attention output is the weighted sum of the values:

$$ \text{Attention}(q, \mathcal{D}) \triangleq \sum_{i=1}^{m} \alpha(q, k_i) v_i $$

Where $\alpha(q, k_i)$ is the attention weight. For a standard Convex Combination in deep learning, $\alpha$ forms a probability distribution:

$$ \sum_{i=1}^{m} \alpha(q, k_i) = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \alpha(q, k_i) \ge 0 $$

The mechanism "pays attention" to the values $v_i$ for which the corresponding query-key similarity score $\alpha$ is high.

Scaled Dot-Product Attention: Derivation

We derive the scoring function from a Gaussian Kernel $a(q, k_i) = -\frac{1}{2}||q - k_i||^2$.

$$ a(q, k_i) = q^T k_i - \frac{1}{2}||k_i||^2 - \frac{1}{2}||q||^2 $$

When normalized via softmax, the $-\frac{1}{2}||q||^2$ term cancels out. Assuming constant key norms, we drop $-\frac{1}{2}||k_i||^2$, leaving the raw dot product: $q^T k_i$.

The Master Equation
$$ \text{Output} = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V $$
Variance Control & Gradient Stability

Assume query $q, k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}$ are independent variables with $\mu=0, \sigma^2=1$.

The variance of their dot product $q^T k = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i$ grows with dimension:

$$ \text{Var}(q^T k) = d_k $$

Large variance pushes softmax into regions with near-zero gradients ($\frac{\partial}{\partial x} \text{softmax} \to 0$). Scaling by $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}$ rigorously forces the variance back to 1.

4. Transformer Architecture: Multi-Head Attention

Sentences exhibit multiple concurrent relationship geometries (syntax, semantics, coreference). A single attention mechanism averages these out. Multi-Head Attention creates parallel representation subspaces.

Subspace Projections

Original $Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_{model}}$ are projected into $h$ lower-dimensional subspaces via learned matrices $W_i^Q, W_i^K, W_i^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{model} \times d_k}$:

$$ Q_i = QW_i^Q, \quad K_i = KW_i^K, \quad V_i = VW_i^V $$
Parallel Execution & Concatenation

Attention is independently computed for each head $i \in \{1, \dots, h\}$, then concatenated and projected via $W^O \in \mathbb{R}^{hd_v \times d_{model}}$:

$$ \text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(Q_i, K_i, V_i) $$ $$ \text{Output} = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h)W^O $$

Each head acts as a specialized "Committee Expert", focusing independently on different contextual signals.

The Mathematics of Order: Positional Encodings

Self-attention is mathematically permutation-invariant ($\mathcal{O}(1)$ bag-of-words). To process sequences, we must explicitly inject spatial order into the continuous embeddings $e_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

Sinusoidal Formulation

For position $p$ and dimension $i \in \{0, \dots, d_{model}-1\}$:

$$ PE_{(p, 2i)} = \sin\left(\frac{p}{10000^{2i/d}}\right) $$ $$ PE_{(p, 2i+1)} = \cos\left(\frac{p}{10000^{2i/d}}\right) $$
The Relative Positioning Property

The model easily learns to attend based on relative positions ($\delta$) because $PE_{p+\delta}$ is a linear transformation of $PE_p$. From sum-of-angles identities:

$$ \sin(\alpha + \beta) = \sin(\alpha)\cos(\beta) + \cos(\alpha)\sin(\beta) $$ $$ \cos(\alpha + \beta) = \cos(\alpha)\cos(\beta) - \sin(\alpha)\sin(\beta) $$

Thus, advancing by offset $\delta$ acts as a constant rotation in the embedding space, making relative tracking highly efficient.

Mathematical Walkthrough: Positional Embeddings

Let us calculate the Positional Encoding (PE) for a specific token in a sequence.

Given Parameters
  • Embedding dimension $d_{model} = 4$.
  • Token position $p = 2$ (e.g., the 3rd token, 0-indexed).
  • Dimension indices $i \in \{0, 1\}$.
1. Calculating for $i = 0$ (Dimensions 0 & 1)
$$ PE_{(2, 0)} = \sin\left(\frac{2}{10000^{0/4}}\right) = \sin(2) \approx 0.909 $$ $$ PE_{(2, 1)} = \cos\left(\frac{2}{10000^{0/4}}\right) = \cos(2) \approx -0.416 $$
2. Calculating for $i = 1$ (Dimensions 2 & 3)
$$ PE_{(2, 2)} = \sin\left(\frac{2}{10000^{2/4}}\right) = \sin\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{10000}}\right) = \sin(0.02) \approx 0.020 $$ $$ PE_{(2, 3)} = \cos\left(\frac{2}{10000^{2/4}}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{10000}}\right) = \cos(0.02) \approx 0.999 $$

Final PE Vector for Position 2

$$ PE_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.909 \\ -0.416 \\ 0.020 \\ 0.999 \end{bmatrix} $$

This vector is added directly element-wise to the word embedding vector $e_2 \in \mathbb{R}^4$.

Architectural Stabilizers for Deep Stacking

Training extreme depths (e.g., LLaMA at 126 layers) requires mechanisms to ensure uninterrupted gradient flow and activation stability.

1. Residual ("Skip") Connections

$$ \text{Output} = \text{Sublayer}(x) + x $$
  • Operates as an "information express lane".
  • Prevents original sequence signal from catastrophic distortion after multiple non-linear transformations.
  • Directly combats the vanishing gradient problem.

2. Layer Normalization

Calculated independently for each input vector $x$ over its $H$ hidden units:

$$ \mu = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{i=1}^{H} x_i, \quad \sigma^2 = \frac{1}{H}\sum_{i=1}^{H} (x_i - \mu)^2 $$ $$ \hat{x}_i = \frac{x_i - \mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \epsilon}} $$ $$ y_i = \gamma_i \hat{x}_i + \beta_i \quad \text{(Scale \& Shift)} $$

Smoothes the optimization landscape and enables deep stacking without magnitude explosion.

The Complete Blueprint: Encoder-Decoder Framework

The Encoder Stack (Understanding)

Processes the source sequence into a continuous representation holding bidirectional context.

Encoder Block ($N \times$)
  1. Multi-Head Self-Attention
  2. Add & Layer Normalization
  3. Position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN)
  4. Add & Layer Normalization

The Decoder Stack (Generation)

Generates the target sequence autoregressively, attending to both prior outputs and the encoder's state.

Decoder Block ($N \times$)
  1. Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention
  2. Add & Layer Normalization
  3. Encoder-Decoder Cross-Attention
  4. Add & Layer Normalization
  5. Position-wise FFN
  6. Add & Layer Normalization

Cross-Attention Bridge:
Queries ($Q$) originate from the Decoder.
Keys ($K$) and Values ($V$) originate from the Encoder's final output.

5. Vision Transformers (ViT) & Inductive Biases

Patch Embeddings Formulation

Standard dense self-attention on $224 \times 224$ pixels requires an intractable $(224^2)^2$ computations. We discretize the image into $N$ patches:

$$ N = \frac{H \times W}{P \times P} = \frac{224 \times 224}{16 \times 16} = 196 $$
The Convolutional Shortcut

Patch unrolling and linear projection are executed efficiently in one step via a single 2D Convolution:

  • Kernel Size = Patch Size $P$ ($16$)
  • Stride = Patch Size $P$ ($16$)
  • Out Channels = Embedding Dim $D$ ($768$)

The [CLS] Token & Biases

A learnable global token $z_0 \in \mathbb{R}^D$ is prepended: $[z_0, z_1, \dots, z_N]$. It synthesizes global spatial features for the final MLP classifier.

Property CNNs ViTs
Inductive Bias Strong (locality, 2D structure) Weak (learns from scratch)
Processing Local receptive fields Global $\mathcal{O}(1)$ attention
Data Scaling Highly sample-efficient Requires immense pre-training (JFT-300M)

Mathematical Walkthrough: ViT Patching Dimensionality

Let us mathematically compute the transformation of a 2D image into a 1D Transformer sequence.

1. Given Parameters
  • Input Image: $H = 224, W = 224$, Channels $C = 3$ (RGB).
  • Patch Size: $P = 16$.
  • Target Embedding Dimension: $D = 768$.
2. Calculating Number of Patches ($N$)

The image is divided into a non-overlapping grid.

$$ N = \frac{H \times W}{P \times P} = \frac{224 \times 224}{16 \times 16} = 14 \times 14 = 196 $$
3. Flattening and Linear Projection

Each patch contains $16 \times 16 \times 3 = 768$ raw pixel values. A learned weight matrix $E \in \mathbb{R}^{(P^2C) \times D}$ projects these into the embedding space:

$$ \text{Raw Patches } \mathbb{R}^{196 \times 768} \xrightarrow{E} \text{Embeddings } \mathbb{R}^{196 \times 768} $$
4. Final Sequence Geometry with [CLS]

We prepend the learnable classification token $z_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$. The final tensor shape fed into the Transformer Encoder becomes:

$$ \text{Shape} = (196 + 1) \times 768 = \mathbf{197 \times 768} $$

6. Architectural Divergence: BERT vs. GPT

Modern Generative AI relies on dismantling the original Encoder-Decoder architecture into specialized halves optimized for unique probabilistic goals.

Model Architecture Attention Visibility Pre-Training Objective
BERT Encoder-Only Bidirectional: Computes $P(w_i | w_{i})$ Masked Language Modeling (MLM): Reconstruct 15% randomly corrupted tokens from full context.
GPT Decoder-Only Causal (Masked): Strictly Unidirectional $P(w_t | w_{ Autoregressive Next-Token Prediction: Maximize log-likelihood of the exact next step.
BERT (Understanding / NLU)

Superior for Sentiment Analysis, NER, and Extractive QA due to its ability to contextualize tokens globally across the entire sequence geometry.

GPT / LLMs (Generation / NLG)

The foundation of modern chat models. By strictly predicting the sequence autoregressively, it is mathematically poised for limitless generative continuation.

Train autoregressively on trillions of tokens (Web crawls). The model learns foundational syntax, world facts, and reasoning geometries natively.
